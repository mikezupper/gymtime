The cascade, layered at last

For most of its life, the cascade was something authors fought rather than used. Specificity wars bred entire methodologies — BEM's underscored incantations, utility prefixes, the nuclear !important — all of them elaborate treaties with an algorithm nobody trusted. Then @layer arrived and made precedence something you declare in one line instead of something you reverse-engineer at midnight.

This page is the argument made concrete: each of its four designs lives in its own cascade layer, and the order of a single declaration at the top of the stylesheet decides every conflict before it happens. The cascade stopped being weather and became architecture.

Layouts that think for themselves

We once measured the viewport in JavaScript, resized boxes by hand, and shipped libraries whose whole job was arithmetic. Grid ended the first era; container queries ended the second. A component now asks how much room do I have? instead of what device am I on? — and the difference is a layout that composes instead of one that breaks.

The garden you are reading is a single grid whose grid-template-areas are just a custom property. Swap the token, and a centered essay becomes a sidebar terminal or an exposed concrete slab — same landmarks, different building. The markup never heard about any of it.

Color grows perceptual

Hex codes were coordinates in a machine's color space, not a designer's. oklch() changed the axes to the ones human eyes actually use — lightness that looks even, chroma that saturates honestly, hue that rotates without surprise mud. Add color-mix() and relative color syntax, and a whole palette becomes a few tokens plus arithmetic.

Every color on this page — cream paper, safety orange, phosphor green, aurora violet — is derived in oklch from a handful of bases, and light-dark() lets the serene garden follow your operating system into the night without a duplicate token set.

Motion leaves the main thread

Animation used to mean a scroll listener, a timestamp, and a prayer for sixty frames. The platform absorbed the whole genre: @starting-style animates entrances, view transitions morph whole states, and scroll-driven timelines run on the compositor while your JavaScript thread does nothing at all.

Just as important, motion learned manners. One media query — prefers-reduced-motion — gates every flourish in this garden, so the blinking cursor, the drifting aurora, and the theme crossfade all yield to a visitor who asked for stillness.

A quiet death for the JavaScript hack

An entire generation of scripts existed to apologize for CSS: Popper for positioning, focus traps for dialogs, checkbox hacks pretending to be state machines. Anchor positioning, <dialog>, popovers, and :has() retired them one by one — declaratively, accessibly, for free.

The theme switcher above is the proof. Four radio buttons, one :root:has() selector, and this entire page re-architects itself — layout, typography, color, and motion — without a single event listener. What we used to call an application is, increasingly, just a stylesheet with opinions.

For twenty years we wrote JavaScript to apologize for CSS. The apologies are over.
